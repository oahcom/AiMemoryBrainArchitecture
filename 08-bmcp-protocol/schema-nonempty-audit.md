## Summary

- Schemas inspected: **2**.
- Constraints inventoried: **93** — 74 `minLength: 1`, 17 `pattern`, and 2 conditional `minProperties: 1`; **0** `minItems`.
- Required array properties reviewed: **14**. Their keys are required, but no `minItems` is imposed, so `[]` remains structurally valid.
- Changed **2** conditional constraints: the previous catch-all `else` branches required highwaters for PARTIAL/NOT_ENUMERATED/UNAVAILABLE/UNKNOWN even when count was zero. They now require a non-empty map only when count is positive or status is COMPLETE_NON_EMPTY. Zero-count incomplete/unavailable states may remain empty.
- Retained **91** `minLength`/`pattern` constraints and **2** narrowed conditional `minProperties` constraints. Added rationale descriptions to all retained constraints.

## Decision rule

A required property is not the same as a non-empty collection. Required array keys may contain `[]`. For highwaters, a positive observed count or COMPLETE_NON_EMPTY status is evidence that at least one item exists; zero-count PARTIAL/NOT_ENUMERATED/UNAVAILABLE/UNKNOWN states may have no reportable highwater and must not synthesize one. Identifiers, digests, counters, signatures and reasons cannot be empty when supplied.

## Scope and limitations

The scan covers JSON Schema files directly in `schemas/`, not recursive subdirectories, schema fragments embedded in Markdown, runtime validators, or external contracts. The guard detects new or changed `minProperties`, `minItems`, `minLength`, and `pattern` declarations. It does not prove that a retained constraint is normatively justified; that requires human contract review. It does not decide semantic validity of empty arrays or infer business meaning beyond schema rules.
